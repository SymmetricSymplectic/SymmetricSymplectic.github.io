What is implemented—and what is being tested

The paper gives a local implied-volatility expansion, not a complete transition-density solver. Its curve is reproduced literally using σATM = σ₀ as an explicit leading-time convention. Maturity affects Black prices and the Monte Carlo experiment; the leading-time analytic smiles themselves are independent of T.

Coefficient comparison and derivation

Set m = log(K/F), q = ν/σ₀. Equations (5.1–5.2) give
σpaper/σ₀ = 1 + (ρq/2)m + [(2−3ρ²)q²/12]m².

The β = 1 SABR small-time expression is σSABR = σ₀ z/χ(z), where z = −qm and χ(z) = log[(√(1−2ρz+z²)+z−ρ)/(1−ρ)]. At z = 0 the continuous value of z/χ(z) is 1. Use the integral representation χ(z) = ∫₀ᶻ (1−2ρu+u²)−1/2du.

Taylor expansion: χ(z) = z + ρz²/2 + (3ρ²−1)z³/6 + O(z⁴), hence the actual inverse is
z/χ(z) = 1 − ρz/2 + (2−3ρ²)z²/12 + O(z³).
Thus the paper’s quadratic coefficient agrees with this β = 1 leading-time formula. The local second derivative is κ = (2−3ρ²)q²/6. The shared sign-change threshold is |ρ| = √(2/3). The graph compares the truncated polynomial with the full leading-time expression, not two different second-order predictions.

Geometry and validity qualifications

With u = eʸ and X = (x−ρu)/√(1−ρ²), the paper’s metric becomes (dX²+du²)/u², so its dimensionless scalar curvature is −2. Multiplication of a metric by 1/ν² multiplies its scalar curvature by ν²: the resulting scalar curvature is −2ν², rather than the −2/ν² stated in Appendix B.

For the multiplicative volatility SDE above, Var(σₜ)/σ₀² = exp(ν²T)−1. Relative volatility excursions are therefore controlled by ν√T, not ν√T/σ₀. The dashboard reports both ν²T and the paper’s criterion ν²T/σ₀² without conflating them. The strike expansion requires |νm/σ₀| small. Neither a small-time expression at long maturity nor a quadratic extrapolation to the wings is an exact option-pricing model.

A Riemannian metric specifies the second-order part of a diffusion generator; it does not by itself supply the pricing measure or all drift terms. The constant-volatility slice is intrinsically flat, but is not a geodesic of the hyperbolic plane. The empirical SPY preset only reproduces the paper’s stated parameter values; no market observations or empirical attractor have been recreated here.

Numerical method and diagnostics

Independent Gaussian increments are generated with a seeded PRNG and Box–Muller. Each step uses the old volatility in Fnext = F exp(−σ²Δt/2 + σ√Δt Z₁), and updates volatility exactly over that interval as σnext = σ exp(−ν²Δt/2 + ν√Δt Z₂), with corr(Z₁,Z₂) = ρ. Forward stepping has time-discretization error. Compare 512 and 1,024 steps to assess it; the plotted confidence intervals quantify sampling uncertainty only.

Call payoffs are averaged directly. Implied volatility is found by bracketed bisection of the Black call formula. Confidence endpoints invert call price ± 1.96 standard errors; infeasible endpoints and unresolved tail prices are omitted and reported. No clipping or rescaling of terminal forwards is used. For positive ρ, continuous β = 1 SABR can be a strict local martingale; finite-step forward means do not establish the continuous martingale property.

Grid diagnostics check price bounds, monotonicity and convexity on the displayed strikes. Passing a finite grid does not prove absence of arbitrage over all strikes or maturities. Negative polynomial volatilities are omitted rather than forced positive.
