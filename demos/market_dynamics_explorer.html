Equations, interpretation, and qualifications

The model and exact variance identity

The continuous system is dx = y dt; dy = (−kx−Ωy) dt + σ dW. The paper’s discrete update at h = 1 is x′ = x+y; y′ = −kx+(1−Ω)y+σε. At general h the demo uses Euler–Maruyama: x′ = x+hy; y′ = −hkx+(1−hΩ)y+σ√h ε. Changing h changes the discrete model.

Write z′ = Mz+η, Cov(η)=Q, and solve Σ = MΣMᵀ+Q. With d = (M₂₁, M₂₂−1), the exact population share is R² = dΣdᵀ/(dΣdᵀ+Q₂₂). At h = 1 this recovers Appendix A:
R² = (Ω²−1.5kΩ+0.5k²+k)/(2Ω−k).
For general Euler h, use K = kh² and O = Ωh in that formula. Noise magnitude cancels because both covariance terms scale as σ²; it still changes the trajectory’s amplitude.

R² measures predictability of the next velocity increment given the current state. It is neither a share of stock-return variance nor evidence of tradable profits. No market data or empirical calibration is recreated here.

Exact sampling of the continuous SDE

Let B = [[0,1],[−k,−Ω]]. The continuous stationary covariance is Σ = diag(σ²/(2kΩ), σ²/(2Ω)). Exact transitions use M = exp(hB) and Q = Σ−MΣMᵀ. Both innovation coordinates are generally nonzero and correlated. The demo samples their full covariance using a Cholesky factor, with no Euler time-stepping error in this mode.

For finite h, E[Δy | z] is the second row of (M−I)z; it is not generally h(−kx−Ωy). As h → 0, the conditional mean variance is O(h²), innovation variance is O(h), and R² → 0. A nonzero fixed variance fraction cannot be assigned to an infinitesimal dy without specifying an observation interval.

Stability and damping: corrections to the draft

For Euler sampling, the necessary and sufficient strict stability conditions are K > 0, O > K, 4−2O+K > 0. At h = 1 these are 0 < k < Ω < 2+k/2. They follow from the Jury conditions for λ²−(2−O)λ+(1−O+K). Appendix A.1 states a broader region that includes unstable matrices; the demo rejects such cases.

Continuous damping is classified by Ω²−4k: negative means underdamped, zero critically damped, positive overdamped. Ω = 1 alone is not a critical-damping condition unless k = 1/4. The exact discrete identity and Ω²/(1+Ω²) have the same value and first derivatives at (k,Ω)=(0,1), but the shared Taylor polynomial is Ω/2. The rational expression is a locally first-order-matching approximation, not literally that Taylor polynomial. At k = 0 the full state lacks a stationary distribution; limits there are formal boundary limits.

What the geometry and Pareto claims do not establish

The algebraic Lyapunov identity is verified directly here. It does not, by itself, prove a universal Pareto frontier. A fixed-k curve can be nondominated within its own restricted family while being dominated when k is also allowed to vary. For example, at Ω = 1.16, the stable choices k = 0.20 and k = 0.26 give different R² at the same damping cost; only the larger value can be nondominated in a set containing both.

As written in §3.1, θ = ds−Ωy dy with constant parameter Ω has dθ = 0, so θ∧dθ = 0. It is therefore not a contact form on (x,y,s). The numerical simulation does not repair or validate this contact-geometric derivation.

The stationary Gaussian covariance depends on (Ω,σ) only through σ²/Ω. Consequently a single-state stationary observation cannot identify all three parameters (k,Ω,σ), and its Fisher metric is singular. A nonsingular information metric requires a specified temporal observation model. The metric quoted in §2.1 cannot be inferred simply from this stationary covariance.

The continuous identity Ω E[y²] = σ²/2 is an energy balance: the mean frictional energy loss equals the mean Itô noise input. Calling Ωy² a total entropy-production rate requires additional thermodynamic definitions; it is not needed for the variance calculation.
