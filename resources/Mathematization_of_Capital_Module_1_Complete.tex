\begin{abstract}
This treatise establishes a unified mathematical formalization of Karl Marx's \textit{Capital}, Volume I, Chapters 1 through 11, organized into three interconnected modules. In \textbf{Module 1 (Chapter 1)}, we construct the dual space of the commodity via graded monoids and probability measure spaces, define the Real Abstraction Operator mapping concrete labor distributions to Socially Necessary Labor Time (SNLT), formalize the non-symmetric Relative-Equivalent Operator, model Commodity Fetishism as a quotient projection over a hidden fiber bundle of social labor relations, and prove the endogenous emergence of the General Equivalent via symmetry-breaking potential game dynamics on the hollow row-stochastic manifold. In \textbf{Module 2 (Chapters 2--3)}, we formalize the exchange process and simple circulation ($C - M - C'$), modeling the \textit{salto mortale} (realization filter), deriving Marx's Law of the Quantity of Circulating Money, and proving a Saddle-Node Crisis Bifurcation Theorem induced by hoarding and liquidity panic. In \textbf{Module 3 (Chapters 4--11)}, we formalize the General Formula for Capital ($M - C - M'$), prove the Conservation of Value in Pure Circulation under arbitrary skew-symmetric bilateral markups, resolve the conservation contradiction via stock-flow operator splitting between Labor-Power and Living Labor, and formulate the differential geometry of the working day and Marx's Three Laws of the Mass of Surplus-Value.
\end{abstract}

\part{Module 1: Axiomatics of the Commodity-Form, Real Abstraction, and Graph Dynamics of the General Equivalent (Volume I, Chapter 1)}

\section{Introduction: Beyond Static Linearism}

The mathematical formalization of Marxian political economy has historically been dominated by the neo-Ricardian or Sraffian tradition (e.g., Bortkiewicz, Seton, Morishima, Okishio), which models production through non-negative Leontief matrices and Perron-Frobenius spectral theory. While powerful for analyzing intersectoral reproduction and the transformation problem of Volume III, linear input-output algebra bypasses the foundational logical movement of Volume I, Chapter 1 (\textit{``The Commodity''}).

In Chapter 1, Marx does not presuppose a pre-existing scalar metric of price or commensurability. Instead, he investigates how a \textbf{homogeneous metric space (Value)} and a \textbf{universal canonical sink (Money)} emerge endogenously out of a \textbf{heterogeneous space of incommensurable physical objects (Use-Values) and private concrete labors}. Formalizing this genesis requires mathematical structures capable of representing qualitative incommensurability, real abstraction, polar asymmetry, and topological symmetry-breaking.

\section{The Dual Space of the Commodity (Chapter 1, Sections 1 \& 2)}

A commodity $c \in \mathcal{C}$ is a dual entity possessing \textbf{Use-Value} (qualitative, incommensurable) and \textbf{Value} (quantitative, commensurable). We represent this duality by constructing two distinct spaces linked by a social abstraction functor.

\subsection{The Physical Use-Value Space ($\mathcal{U}$)}

Let $\mathcal{T} = \{1, 2, \dots, n\}$ be the index set of distinct concrete commodity types (e.g., linen, coats, iron, corn). Because distinct use-values lack a common physical metric ($1\text{ coat} + 1\text{ ton of iron}$ admits no physical scalar sum), the space of physical commodities is constructed as a \textbf{disjoint union of 1-dimensional positive cones} (a Graded Monoid):
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{U} = \bigsqcup_{i \in \mathcal{T}} \mathcal{U}_i, \quad \text{where } \mathcal{U}_i = \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} \cdot \mathbf{e}_i
\end{equation}
Here, $\mathbf{e}_i$ denotes the basis unit of physical measurement for commodity $i$ (yards, tons, bushels). A specific commodity bundle of type $i$ is written as $q_i \mathbf{e}_i \in \mathcal{U}_i$. There is no native inner product, norm, or addition operator on $\mathcal{U}$ that directly combines or compares $\mathbf{e}_i$ and $\mathbf{e}_j$ for $i \neq j$.

\subsection{Concrete vs. Abstract Labor and Socially Necessary Labor Time (SNLT)}

Let $\mathcal{L}_i$ represent the set of individual producers (or production processes) creating commodity $i$. Each producer $\omega \in \mathcal{L}_i$ expends a specific \textbf{concrete labor} characterized by technical vectors (skill, intensity, tool efficiency) and requires individual physical clock-time $\tau_i(\omega) \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}$ to produce one physical unit $\mathbf{e}_i$.

Let $(\mathcal{L}_i, \Sigma_i, \mu_i)$ be a probability measure space over the producers of sector $i$, where $\mu_i(\omega)$ represents the share of total social output of commodity $i$ produced by agent $\omega$, satisfying $\int_{\mathcal{L}_i} d\mu_i(\omega) = 1$.

\begin{definition}[The Real Abstraction Operator $\mathcal{A}$]
Commodity exchange strips away the qualitative specificity of concrete labor, projecting heterogeneous labor processes onto a single one-dimensional scalar manifold: \textbf{Abstract Human Labor} ($\mathcal{V} \cong \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$). The unit value $\lambda_i$ of commodity $i$---its \textbf{Socially Necessary Labor Time (SNLT)}---is defined via the expectation operator over the sectoral productivity distribution, weighted by skill/intensity multipliers $\sigma_i(\omega) \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}$:
\begin{equation}
    \lambda_i = \mathcal{A}(\mathcal{L}_i) \coloneqq \int_{\mathcal{L}_i} \sigma_i(\omega) \tau_i(\omega) \, d\mu_i(\omega)
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Comparative Statics of Technological Shock]
If a technological revolution occurs (e.g., power looms replace handlooms), the measure $\mu_i$ shifts mass toward producers with lower $\tau_i(\omega)$. This immediately depresses $\lambda_i$ across the entire existing inventory of commodity $i$, capturing Marx's insistence that value is a contemporaneous social relation rather than a historical accounting of past effort.
\end{remark}

The \textbf{Total Value Functional} $V: \mathcal{U} \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ acting on a homogeneous commodity bundle $q_i \mathbf{e}_i \in \mathcal{U}_i$ is therefore:
\begin{equation}
    V(q_i \mathbf{e}_i) = q_i \lambda_i
\end{equation}

\section{The Algebra of the Value-Form (Chapter 1, Section 3)}

Marx demonstrates that value ($\lambda_i$) cannot appear directly as an isolated scalar; a commodity cannot express its value in its own physical body. Value requires a \textbf{polar relational mapping} between two distinct commodities.

\subsection{The Value Equivalence Relation ($\sim_V$)}

Define a binary relation $\sim_V$ on the disjoint space $\mathcal{U}$:
\begin{equation}
    q_i \mathbf{e}_i \sim_V q_j \mathbf{e}_j \iff V(q_i \mathbf{e}_i) = V(q_j \mathbf{e}_j) \iff q_i \lambda_i = q_j \lambda_j
\end{equation}
While $\sim_V$ is mathematically an equivalence relation (reflexive, symmetric, transitive) on the underlying scalar space $\mathcal{V}$, \textbf{its phenomenal expression in the commodity world is strictly non-symmetric}. In the value equation ``$x \text{ commodity } A$ is worth $y \text{ commodity } B$,'' $A$ and $B$ occupy polarized structural roles.

\subsection{The Non-Symmetric Value Expression Operator ($\mathcal{E}$)}

\begin{definition}[Relative-Equivalent Operator]
For $i \neq j$, define the operator $\mathcal{E}: \mathcal{U}_i \times \{\mathbf{e}_j\} \to \mathcal{U}_j$, where commodity $i$ occupies the \textbf{Relative Form} (the active subject seeking value-expression) and commodity $j$ occupies the \textbf{Equivalent Form} (the passive physical mirror whose bodily use-value measures $i$):
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{E}(q_i \mathbf{e}_i \mid \mathbf{e}_j) = \left( q_i \frac{\lambda_i}{\lambda_j} \right) \mathbf{e}_j \in \mathcal{U}_j
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Two fundamental formal properties directly encode Marx's dialectical exposition:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item \textbf{Irreflexivity of Phenomenal Expression:} For $i = j$, $\mathcal{E}(q_i \mathbf{e}_i \mid \mathbf{e}_i) = q_i \mathbf{e}_i$, which reduces to a meaningless tautology ($20\text{ yards of linen} = 20\text{ yards of linen}$) and fails to express value. Valid value-expressions are strictly restricted to off-diagonal pairs $(i,j) \in \mathcal{T} \times \mathcal{T}$ with $i \neq j$.
    \item \textbf{Dimensional Projection:} The codomain of $\mathcal{E}(q_i \mathbf{e}_i \mid \mathbf{e}_j)$ is the physical subspace $\mathcal{U}_j$, \textit{not} abstract labor hours $\mathcal{V}$. Abstract value $\lambda_i$ becomes phenomenally visible only by projecting $i$ onto the concrete physical metric of $j$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Topological Evolution of the Four Forms of Value}

We formalize the progression in Chapter 1, Section 3 as a sequence of \textbf{directed graphs} $G_k = (\mathcal{T}, E_k)$, where a directed edge $(i \to j) \in E_k$ signifies that commodity $i$ (Relative Form) expresses its value in the physical body of commodity $j$ (Equivalent Form):

\paragraph{Dialectical Dualization (Form II $\to$ Form III):} In graph-theoretic and categorical terms, Form III is the \textbf{transpose (dual graph)} of Form II: $G_{\text{III}} = G_{\text{II}}^\top$. By inverting the rays of the Out-Star so that all $k \in \mathcal{T} \setminus \{u\}$ point into a single excluded commodity $u$, the disjoint space $\mathcal{U}$ acquires a canonical isomorphism to $\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ mediated by the physical basis unit $\mathbf{e}_u$.

\section{Commodity Fetishism as a Quotient Topology (Chapter 1, Section 4)}

In Section 4, Marx introduces \textbf{Commodity Fetishism}: the social relations among private producers $(\mathcal{L}_1, \dots, \mathcal{L}_n)$ do not appear directly as transparent social relations; instead, they appear exclusively as quantitative exchange-ratios (prices) attached to things.

We formalize this via an \textbf{epimorphism (projection) and a hidden fiber bundle}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Let $\Omega = \prod_{i \in \mathcal{T}} \mathcal{L}_i$ be the \textbf{Total Social Labor Configuration Space}, encoding the complete network of the social division of labor, concrete production relations, and productivity distributions $\mu_i$.
    \item Let $\mathcal{P} = \mathbb{RP}^{n-1}_{>0}$ be the \textbf{Projective Price Simplex} of observable exchange ratios $[p_1 : p_2 : \dots : p_n]$, where $p_i = \lambda_i / \lambda_m$.
    \item The market exchange mechanism acts as a projection map $\Pi: \Omega \to \mathcal{P}$.
\end{enumerate}

Because $\dim(\Omega) \gg \dim(\mathcal{P})$, $\Pi$ is a non-injective surjection with high-dimensional fibers $\Pi^{-1}(\mathbf{p})$. Private producers in a commodity-producing society cannot observe $\Omega$ directly; they receive feedback signals exclusively from $\mathcal{P}$ (fluctuations in relative prices) and reallocate their concrete labor accordingly. \textbf{Fetishism} is the structural illusion induced by the projection $\Pi$, whereby invariants of the fiber space $\Omega$ (social relations of production) are misattributed to the base manifold $\mathcal{P}$ (natural properties of physical commodities).

\section{Directed Graph Dynamics: Proving the Emergence of the General Equivalent}

In Chapter 1, Section 3 (and Chapter 2, ``The Process of Exchange''), Marx identifies a fundamental logical and practical contradiction between the \textbf{Expanded Form of Value ($G_{\text{II}}$)} and the requirements of a coherent commodity-producing society. If every commodity simultaneously attempts to exclude all others as its private out-star, no commodity is excluded, and social commensurability collapses into $O(n^2)$ non-transitive bilateral ratios. Consequently, the ``social action of all other commodities'' (\textit{gesellschaftliche Tat}) must spontaneously break the symmetry of the commodity space $\mathcal{T}$ and isolate a unique sink $u^* \in \mathcal{T}$---transforming the collection of Out-Stars ($G_{\text{II}}$) into a single \textbf{In-Star topology ($G_{\text{III}}$)}.

\subsection{The Hollow Row-Stochastic Manifold of Value-Expression Networks}

Let $\mathcal{T} = \{1, 2, \dots, n\}$ ($n \ge 3$) be the set of distinct commodity types, with positive unit values $\boldsymbol{\lambda} = (\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n) \in \mathbb{R}^n_{>0}$.

\begin{definition}[Value-Expression Adjacency Manifold]
At time $t \ge 0$, each commodity sector $i \in \mathcal{T}$ allocates weights $w_{ij}(t) \in [0, 1]$ across the other $n-1$ commodities to serve as its \textbf{Equivalent Form}. Because the Relative-Equivalent Operator $\mathcal{E}(q_i \mathbf{e}_i \mid \mathbf{e}_j)$ is strictly irreflexive ($i \neq j$), the weighted directed graph $G(t) = (\mathcal{T}, W(t))$ evolves on the \textbf{hollow row-stochastic manifold}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{W} = \left\{ W \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}_{\ge 0} \;\middle|\; w_{ii} = 0 \text{ for all } i \in \mathcal{T}, \text{ and } \sum_{j \neq i} w_{ij} = 1 \text{ for all } i \in \mathcal{T} \right\}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Equivalent Prominence / Normalized In-Degree]
For any state $W \in \mathcal{W}$, define the \textbf{Equivalent Prominence} of commodity $j \in \mathcal{T}$ as:
\begin{equation}
    d_j(W) \coloneqq \frac{1}{n-1} \sum_{k \neq j} w_{kj} \in [0, 1]
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Micro-Foundations and the Global Social Commensurability Potential}

When commodity $i$ expresses its value in commodity $j$ ($i \to j$), that expression achieves social commensurability to the extent that other commodities $k \notin \{i, j\}$ also recognize $j$ as their equivalent ($k \to j$). When both $i \to j$ and $k \to j$ hold, commodity $j$ mediates a convergent two-step wedge $i \to j \leftarrow k$ in the same physical subspace $\mathcal{U}_j$:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\mathcal{E}(q_i \mathbf{e}_i \mid \mathbf{e}_j)}{\mathcal{E}(q_k \mathbf{e}_k \mid \mathbf{e}_j)} = \frac{q_i \lambda_i / \lambda_j}{q_k \lambda_k / \lambda_j} = \frac{q_i \lambda_i}{q_k \lambda_k}
\end{equation}
Thus, the strategic payoff to sector $i$ from allocating weight $w_{ij}$ to equivalent $j$ is given by:
\begin{equation}
    u_{ij}(W) = \alpha \sum_{k \notin \{i, j\}} w_{kj} + \eta_j
\end{equation}
where $\alpha > 0$ measures the \textbf{social commensurability coupling} and $\eta_j \ge 0$ represents physical suitability for the equivalent role (divisibility, durability, uniformity).

\begin{lemma}[Existence of the Social Commensurability Potential]
The $n$-sector value-expression system on $\mathcal{W} = \prod_{i=1}^n \Delta_i$ is an \textbf{Exact Potential Game} with global potential functional $\Phi: \mathcal{W} \to \mathbb{R}$ defined by:
\begin{equation}
    \Phi(W) \coloneqq \frac{\alpha}{2} \sum_{j=1}^n \sum_{i \neq j} \sum_{k \notin \{i, j\}} w_{ij} w_{kj} + \sum_{j=1}^n \eta_j \sum_{i \neq j} w_{ij} = \frac{\alpha}{2} \sum_{j=1}^n \left[ \left(\sum_{i \neq j} w_{ij}\right)^2 - \sum_{i \neq j} w_{ij}^2 \right] + \sum_{j=1}^n \eta_j \left(\sum_{i \neq j} w_{ij}\right)
\end{equation}
satisfying $\frac{\partial \Phi(W)}{\partial w_{ij}} = u_{ij}(W)$ for all $i \neq j$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Direct partial differentiation of $\Phi(W)$ with respect to $w_{ij}$ ($i \neq j$) isolates the $j$-th summand and yields $\frac{\partial \Phi(W)}{\partial w_{ij}} = \alpha \sum_{k \notin \{i, j\}} w_{kj} + \eta_j = u_{ij}(W)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Replicator Dynamics and the Symmetry-Breaking Theorem}

Each commodity sector $i \in \mathcal{T}$ updates its equivalent weights $w_{ij}(t)$ via continuous-time multi-population replicator dynamics:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{w}_{ij} = w_{ij} \left( u_{ij}(W) - \bar{u}_i(W) \right), \quad \forall i \in \mathcal{T}, \; j \neq i, \quad \text{where } \bar{u}_i(W) = \sum_{l \neq i} w_{il} u_{il}(W)
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Instability of the Expanded Form $G_{\text{II}}$ and Global Convergence to the General Form $G_{\text{III}}$]
Assume pure initial symmetry among commodities ($\eta_1 = \eta_2 = \dots = \eta_n = 0$) and $\alpha > 0$. Under the replicator dynamics on $\mathcal{W}$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
    \item \textbf{Strict Lyapunov Ascent:} $\Phi(W(t))$ is strictly non-decreasing along trajectories:
    \begin{equation}
        \frac{d}{dt} \Phi(W(t)) = \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j \neq i} w_{ij} \left(u_{ij}(W) - \bar{u}_i(W)\right)^2 \ge 0
    \end{equation}
    with equality if and only if $W$ is a stationary point.
    \item \textbf{Linear Instability of the Expanded Form ($W^{\text{II}}$):} The symmetric Expanded Form centroid $W^{\text{II}}$ ($w_{ij} = \frac{1}{n-1}$) is dynamically unstable. Any perturbation $S_j = \sum_{i \neq j} \epsilon_{ij}$ to the total in-degree of commodity $j$ grows exponentially with strictly positive eigenvalue:
    \begin{equation}
        \mu_{\text{star}} = \alpha \left( \frac{n^2 - 3n + 1}{(n-1)^2} \right) > 0 \quad (\forall n \ge 3)
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Global Topological Collapse to the In-Star ($G_{\text{III}}$):} For almost all initial conditions $W(0) \in \operatorname{int}(\mathcal{W})$, the trajectory $W(t)$ converges as $t \to \infty$ to a corner configuration $W^{\text{III}(u^*)}$ where a single commodity $u^* \in \mathcal{T}$ emerges as the unique General Equivalent:
    \begin{equation}
        \lim_{t \to \infty} w_{i u^*}(t) = 1 \quad \forall i \in \mathcal{T} \setminus \{u^*\}, \quad \text{and} \quad \lim_{t \to \infty} d_{u^*}(W(t)) = 1
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{Part (1):} By the chain rule and Lemma 5.1, $\dot{\Phi} = \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j \neq i} u_{ij} w_{ij} (u_{ij} - \bar{u}_i)$. Subtracting $\sum_{i=1}^n \bar{u}_i \sum_{j \neq i} w_{ij}(u_{ij} - \bar{u}_i) = 0$ yields $\dot{\Phi} = \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j \neq i} w_{ij}(u_{ij} - \bar{u}_i)^2 \ge 0$. By compactness of $\mathcal{W}$ and LaSalle's Invariance Principle, all trajectories converge to the stationary locus $\{\dot{\Phi} = 0\}$.

\textbf{Part (2):} Linearizing around $w^{\text{II}}_{ij} = \frac{1}{n-1}$ with $w_{ij} = \frac{1}{n-1} + \epsilon_{ij}$ ($\epsilon_{ii} = 0$, $\sum_{j \neq i} \epsilon_{ij} = 0$) and defining $S_j = \sum_{k \neq j} \epsilon_{kj}$ ($\sum_{j=1}^n S_j = 0$), the perturbed payoff is $\delta u_{ij} = \alpha (S_j - \epsilon_{ij})$. Summing the linearized replicator equation over $i \neq j$ gives:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{S}_j = \frac{\alpha}{n-1} \sum_{i \neq j} \left[ (S_j - \epsilon_{ij}) - \frac{1}{n-1} \sum_{l \neq i} (S_l - \epsilon_{il}) \right] = \frac{\alpha}{n-1} \left[ (n-2) S_j - \frac{1}{n-1} S_j \right] = \alpha \left( \frac{n^2 - 3n + 1}{(n-1)^2} \right) S_j
\end{equation}
Since $n^2 - 3n + 1 \ge 1 > 0$ for all $n \ge 3$, $\mu_{\text{star}} > 0$, proving instability of $W^{\text{II}}$.

\textbf{Part (3):} A rest point $W^*$ is asymptotically stable under replicator dynamics of a potential game if and only if it is a strict local maximizer of $\Phi(W)$. Along any coordinated multi-agent interior perturbation where two agents $i_1, i_2$ shift weight $\pm \delta$ between two distinct equivalents $j_1, j_2$, the second variation of $\Phi$ is $\delta^2 \Phi = 4\alpha \delta^2 > 0$. Thus, no interior or partially mixed multi-sink state can be a local maximum. At pure vertices where each $i$ selects a unique target $f(i) \neq i$ with in-degrees $k_j = |f^{-1}(j)| \in \{0, \dots, n-1\}$ ($\sum_j k_j = n$), $\Phi(W) = \frac{\alpha}{2} \sum_{j=1}^n k_j(k_j - 1)$. By strict convexity of $k(k-1)$, $\Phi$ is uniquely maximized when a single sink $u^*$ achieves $k_{u^*} = n-1$, one node $v = f(u^*)$ has $k_v = 1$, and all other $n-2$ nodes have $k_j = 0$, yielding $\max \Phi = \frac{\alpha}{2}(n-1)(n-2)$ at $W^{\text{III}(u^*)}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Polar Exclusion of the General Equivalent and Transition to the Money-Form $G_{\text{IV}}$]
At the General Form attractor $W^{\text{III}(u^*)}$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item \textbf{Exclusion from the Relative Form:} For the General Equivalent $u^*$ itself, $u_{u^* v}(W^{\text{III}(u^*)}) = \alpha \sum_{k \notin \{u^*, v\}} w_{kv} = 0$ for all $v \neq u^*$, so $\nabla_{\mathbf{w}_{u^*}} \Phi = \mathbf{0}$ (when $\boldsymbol{\eta} = \mathbf{0}$). Thus, $u^*$ is structurally excluded from the active Relative Form; its value can only be read by transposing the In-Star $G_{\text{III}}$ back into the Out-Star $G_{\text{II}}$.
    \item \textbf{Symmetry Breaking to the Money-Form ($G_{\text{IV}}$):} When physical suitability $\boldsymbol{\eta}$ has a unique maximum $\eta_m > \eta_j$ ($\forall j \neq m$, e.g., Gold), the $S_n$ permutation degeneracy is broken, uniquely selecting $u^* = m$ as the global maximizer $\Phi(W^{\text{III}(m)}) = \frac{\alpha}{2}(n-1)(n-2) + (n-1)\eta_m$ and establishing the canonical price vector $p_i = \lambda_i / \lambda_m$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\part{Module 2: Stochastic Exchange, the \textit{Salto Mortale}, and the Metamorphosis $C - M - C'$ (Volume I, Chapters 2 \& 3)}

\section{The Mutual Exclusion Principle and Bipartite Exchange Polarity (Chapter 2)}

In Chapter 1, unit value $\lambda_i$ and money-price $p_i = \lambda_i / \lambda_m$ are derived as ideal expectations over the production measure $\mu_i$. In Chapters 2 and 3, a commodity bundle $q_i \mathbf{e}_i$ enters the market bearing \textbf{potential (ideal) value} $V^{\text{pot}}_i = q_i \lambda_i$, which must be validated as actual social labor through the stochastic realization process $C_i \to M$ (\textit{salto mortale}).

\begin{definition}[The Agent-Commodity State Tuple]
Let $\mathcal{A} = \{1, 2, \dots, N\}$ be the population of private commodity owners. At time $t$, agent $\alpha \in \mathcal{A}$ is characterized by a state tuple:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{S}_\alpha(t) = \left( \mathbf{q}_\alpha(t), \, m_\alpha(t), \, \mathbf{u}_\alpha \right) \in \mathcal{U} \times \mathcal{U}_m \times \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{T}_0)
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{q}_\alpha(t) = q_{\alpha, i} \mathbf{e}_i \in \mathcal{U}_i$ is the physical commodity bundle produced by $\alpha$, $m_\alpha(t) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} \cdot \mathbf{e}_m$ is agent $\alpha$'s holding of Money, and $\mathbf{u}_\alpha: \mathcal{T}_0 \to \{0, 1\}$ is a binary concrete need indicator over ordinary commodities $\mathcal{T}_0 = \mathcal{T} \setminus \{m\}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{axiom}[The Non-Use-Value Condition of Commodity Production]
For any commodity producer $\alpha \in \mathcal{A}$ producing sector $i \in \mathcal{T}_0$:
\begin{equation}
    u_\alpha(i) = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \exists j \in \mathcal{T}_0 \setminus \{i\} \text{ such that } u_\alpha(j) = 1
\end{equation}
That is, one's own product is a pure bearer of exchange-value (a non-use-value to its owner) and must be alienated to an agent $\beta$ for whom $u_\beta(i) = 1$.
\end{axiom}

\section{The \textit{Salto Mortale} and the Realization Operator (Chapter 3, Section 2a)}

In Chapter 3, Section 2a, Marx introduces the \textbf{second dimension of Socially Necessary Labor Time}: even if every unit of commodity $i$ is produced in the technically necessary time $\lambda_i$, if aggregate effective social demand cannot absorb the total sector output $Q_i = \sum_{\alpha \in \mathcal{A}_i} q_{\alpha, i}$, the excess labor time expended in sector $i$ fails to realize as social value.

\begin{definition}[Produced Ideal Value vs. Effective Social Demand]
For each sector $i \in \mathcal{T}_0$ over a market clearing period $[t, t+\Delta t]$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item \textbf{Total Produced Ideal Value:} $V^{\text{prod}}_i = \lambda_i Q_i = \lambda_i \sum_{\alpha \in \mathcal{A}_i} q_{\alpha, i}$.
    \item \textbf{Stochastic Effective Monetary Demand:} Let $M^{\text{circ}}_i(\omega_t) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ be a random variable on a filtered market probability space $(\Xi, \mathcal{F}, (\mathcal{F}_t)_{t \ge 0}, \mathbb{P})$ representing the total quantity of money $\mathbf{e}_m$ directed by buyers toward sector $i$. In units of abstract labor time, the socially allocated demand is $D_i(\omega_t) = \lambda_m M^{\text{circ}}_i(\omega_t)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The \textit{Salto Mortale} Realization Operator $\mathcal{R}_i$]
The transition from ideal commodity value $C_i$ to realized money $M$ is governed by a non-linear operator $\mathcal{R}_i: \mathcal{U}_i \times \Xi \to \mathcal{U}_m$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
    \item \textbf{Quantity-Rationing Regime (Fixed Price $p_i = \lambda_i / \lambda_m$):} Each unit of commodity $i$ faces a realization survival probability:
    \begin{equation}
        \pi_i(\omega_t) = \min\!\left(1, \; \frac{D_i(\omega_t)}{V^{\text{prod}}_i}\right) = \min\!\left(1, \; \frac{\lambda_m M^{\text{circ}}_i(\omega_t)}{\lambda_i Q_i}\right) \in [0, 1]
    \end{equation}
    yielding realized value $V^{\text{real}}_{\alpha, i}(\omega_t) = \lambda_m \mathcal{R}_i(q_{\alpha, i}\mathbf{e}_i, \omega_t) = \pi_i(\omega_t) q_{\alpha, i} \lambda_i \le V^{\text{pot}}_{\alpha, i}$, with residual $(1 - \pi_i(\omega_t)) q_{\alpha, i} \lambda_i$ representing devalued labor time.
    \item \textbf{Market-Price Divergence Regime (Flexible Price $\tilde{p}_i(\omega_t) = M^{\text{circ}}_i(\omega_t) / Q_i$):} The market-validated unit labor value shifts to:
    \begin{equation}
        \tilde{\lambda}_i(\omega_t) = \lambda_m \tilde{p}_i(\omega_t) = \lambda_i \left(\frac{D_i(\omega_t)}{V^{\text{prod}}_i}\right)
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\section{Operator Algebra of the Circuit $C_i - M - C_j$}

Simple commodity circulation decomposes into two antithetical operator phases:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{First Metamorphosis (Sale, $C_i \to M$):} The stochastic Alienation Operator $\hat{\mathcal{S}}_i: \mathcal{U}_i \to \mathcal{U}_m$:
    \begin{equation}
        \hat{\mathcal{S}}_i(q_i \mathbf{e}_i) = \left( \pi_i(\omega_t) q_i \frac{\lambda_i}{\lambda_m} \right) \mathbf{e}_m
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Second Metamorphosis (Purchase, $M \to C_j$):} The Appropriation Operator $\hat{\mathcal{B}}_j: \mathcal{U}_m \to \mathcal{U}_j$:
    \begin{equation}
        \hat{\mathcal{B}}_j(m \mathbf{e}_m) = \left( m \frac{\lambda_m}{\lambda_j} \right) \mathbf{e}_j
    \end{equation}
\end{itemize}

\begin{proposition}[Topological Asymmetry of Sale $\hat{\mathcal{S}}_i$ and Purchase $\hat{\mathcal{B}}_j$]
In the Money-Form topology $G_{\text{IV}}$ (In-Star with sink $m$), Sale ($\hat{\mathcal{S}}_i$) is a stochastic contraction ($\pi_i(\omega_t) \le 1$) because ordinary commodities $i \in \mathcal{T}_0$ occupy the Relative Form and lack direct universal exchangeability. Conversely, Purchase ($\hat{\mathcal{B}}_j$) traverses the transposed Out-Star $G_{\text{IV}}^\top$ from the Universal Equivalent sink $m$, possessing unconditional realizability ($\pi_m \equiv 1$ whenever inventories $Q_j > 0$ exist). The composite circuit operator $\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{i \to j} \coloneqq \hat{\mathcal{B}}_j \circ \hat{\mathcal{S}}_i: \mathcal{U}_i \to \mathcal{U}_j$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathcal{M}}_{i \to j}(q_i \mathbf{e}_i) = \left( \pi_i(\omega_t) q_i \frac{\lambda_i}{\lambda_j} \right) \mathbf{e}_j, \quad \mathbb{E}\!\left[V\!\left(\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{i \to j}(q_i \mathbf{e}_i)\right)\right] \le V(q_i \mathbf{e}_i)
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\section{Breaking Say's Law: Markov Circulation Dynamics and the Crisis Bifurcation (Chapter 3, Sections 2b \& 3)}

Because $C_i \to M$ and $M \to C_j$ are separated in time by an intermediate holding state $M$, an agent who has sold ($C_i \to M$) is under no compulsion to immediately buy ($M \to C_j$). Money can immobilize itself as a \textbf{Hoard} (\textit{Schatzbildung}).

\begin{definition}[Four-State Circulation Markov Process]
Social value in circulation transitions across four states $\Sigma = \{\mathbf{C}, \mathbf{M}_{\text{circ}}, \mathbf{M}_{\text{hoard}}, \mathbf{K}\}$ with macro-distribution $\mathbf{x}(t) = (x_C(t), x_M(t), x_H(t), x_K(t))^\top$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
    \item $\mathbf{C}$ (\textbf{Commodity Capital on Market}): Realizes into $\mathbf{M}_{\text{circ}}$ at rate $\gamma_{\text{sale}}(x_M) = \beta x_M$ ($\beta > 0$), or decays into crisis/default state $\mathbf{K}$ at expiration hazard rate $\delta_{\text{crisis}} > 0$.
    \item $\mathbf{M}_{\text{circ}}$ (\textbf{Active Circulating Medium}): Executes purchases $\mathbf{M}_{\text{circ}} \to \mathbf{C}$ at rate $\mu_{\text{buy}} > 0$, or withdraws into hoards $\mathbf{M}_{\text{hoard}}$ at rate $\sigma_{\text{hoard}} \ge 0$.
    \item $\mathbf{M}_{\text{hoard}}$ (\textbf{Hoarded Money / Liquidity Reserve}): Re-enters active circulation $\mathbf{M}_{\text{hoard}} \to \mathbf{M}_{\text{circ}}$ at dishoarding rate $\rho_{\text{dishoard}} > 0$.
    \item $\mathbf{K}$ (\textbf{Crisis / Unrealized Default State}): Absorbing or distressed inventory state resulting from failed realization.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Marx's Law of the Quantity of Circulating Money]
Let $P_{\text{sum}}(t) = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{T}_0} p_i Q_i(t) = \frac{1}{\lambda_m} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{T}_0} \lambda_i Q_i(t)$ be the sum of prices of commodities in circulation over period $T$, $K_{\text{credit}}(t)$ be mutual credit offsets, $D_{\text{due}}(t)$ be maturing debt obligations, and $v$ be the velocity of money. Then the required active circulating money stock is:
\begin{equation}
    M^{\text{active}}(t) = \frac{P_{\text{sum}}(t) - K_{\text{credit}}(t) + D_{\text{due}}(t)}{v} = \frac{1}{v \lambda_m} \left( \sum_{i \in \mathcal{T}_0} \lambda_i Q_i(t) - \lambda_m K_{\text{credit}}(t) + \lambda_m D_{\text{due}}(t) \right)
\end{equation}
Unlike neoclassical monetarism ($MV = PY$), prices $p_i = \lambda_i / \lambda_m$ are determined prior to circulation by the production measure $\mu_i$, and fluctuations in required $M^{\text{active}}(t)$ are buffered by the hoard reservoir $M^{\text{hoard}}(t) = M^{\text{total}} - M^{\text{active}}(t)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Breakability of $C - M - C'$ and the Realization Crisis Phase Transition]
Let total money be normalized to $M^{\text{total}} = x_M(t) + x_H(t) = M_0$. Under the hoarding dynamics $\dot{x}_M = \rho_{\text{dishoard}}(M_0 - x_M) - \sigma_{\text{hoard}} x_M$, the steady-state active money stock is $x_M^*(h) = \frac{M_0}{1 + h}$, where $h \coloneqq \sigma_{\text{hoard}} / \rho_{\text{dishoard}}$ is the \textbf{Hoarding Propensity Ratio}, and the \textbf{Global Realization Efficiency} is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{E}_{\text{real}}(h) = \frac{\gamma_{\text{sale}}(x_M^*)}{\gamma_{\text{sale}}(x_M^*) + \delta_{\text{crisis}}} = \frac{\beta M_0}{\beta M_0 + \delta_{\text{crisis}}(1 + h)}
\end{equation}
Suppose hoarding is endogenously coupled to realization failure via credit-default panic: $h(u) = h_0 + \tilde{\xi} u^2$, where $u \coloneqq 1 - \mathcal{E}_{\text{real}} \in [0, 1]$ is the unrealized crisis share and $\tilde{\xi} = \xi / \rho_{\text{dishoard}} > 0$ is panic sensitivity. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item Every equilibrium $u^* \in (0, 1)$ satisfies the fixed-point balance equation:
    \begin{equation}
        \underbrace{\frac{\beta M_0}{\delta_{\text{crisis}}} \frac{u}{1 - u}}_{L(u)} = \underbrace{1 + h_0 + \tilde{\xi} u^2}_{R(u)}
    \end{equation}
    \item As $\tilde{\xi}$ crosses the critical threshold $\tilde{\xi}_c$ defined by the tangency $L(u_c) = R(u_c)$ and $L'(u_c) = R'(u_c)$, the circulation system undergoes a \textbf{saddle-node bifurcation}, generating a stable \textbf{Acute Monetary Crisis / Liquidity Trap Attractor} ($u_3^* \to 1$, $x_M^* \to 0$) even prior to capitalist production.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Substituting $x_M^* = \frac{M_0}{1 + h(u)}$ into $u = 1 - \mathcal{E}_{\text{real}} = \frac{\delta_{\text{crisis}}}{\beta x_M^* + \delta_{\text{crisis}}}$ yields $u = \frac{\delta_{\text{crisis}}(1 + h_0 + \tilde{\xi} u^2)}{\beta M_0 + \delta_{\text{crisis}}(1 + h_0 + \tilde{\xi} u^2)}$. Rearranging algebraically isolates $L(u) = \frac{\beta M_0}{\delta_{\text{crisis}}} \frac{u}{1-u}$ on the left and $R(u) = 1 + h_0 + \tilde{\xi} u^2$ on the right. Since $L(0) = 0 < R(0) = 1 + h_0$ and $\lim_{u \to 1^-} L(u) = +\infty > R(1)$, at least one root exists in $(0,1)$. Because $L(u)$ is strictly increasing with $L''(u) = \frac{2\beta M_0}{\delta_{\text{crisis}}(1-u)^3}$ and $R(u)$ is quadratic, varying $\tilde{\xi}$ across the tangency condition produces a classic fold (saddle-node) bifurcation with two stable attractors ($u_1^* \ll 1$ and $u_3^* \to 1$) separated by an unstable tipping threshold $u_2^*$.
\end{proof}

\part{Module 3: The Valorization Operator, the Conservation Contradiction, and the Mechanics of Surplus-Value (Volume I, Chapters 4--11)}

\section{Topological Inversion of the Circuit: $C - M - C$ vs. $M - C - M'$ (Chapter 4)}

In Part II of \textit{Capital} (Chapters 4--6), Marx introduces the \textbf{General Formula for Capital}:
\begin{equation}
    M - C - M' \quad \text{where } M' = M + \Delta M, \; \Delta M > 0
\end{equation}
Whereas simple circulation $\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{i \to j} = \hat{\mathcal{B}}_j \circ \hat{\mathcal{S}}_i : \mathcal{U}_i \to \mathcal{U}_j$ maps between qualitatively distinct physical cones under quantitative value equivalence ($V_i = V_j$), the capital circuit $\hat{\mathcal{K}}_i = \hat{\mathcal{S}}'_i \circ \hat{\mathcal{P}} \circ \hat{\mathcal{B}}_i : \mathcal{U}_m \to \mathcal{U}_m$ is an endomorphism on the 1-dimensional Universal Equivalent space $\mathcal{U}_m$. Because its initial and terminal spaces are qualitatively identical ($\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} \cdot \mathbf{e}_m$), the transformation is tautological unless it achieves a strictly positive scalar dilation $\Delta M > 0$ (\textbf{Surplus-Value} $s$).

\section{The Impossibility Theorem of Pure Circulation (Chapter 5)}

In Chapter 5 (\textit{``Contradictions in the General Formula''}), Marx proves that surplus-value ($\Delta V > 0$) cannot arise inside the sphere of circulation, even under arbitrary non-equivalent exchange (``buying cheap and selling dear'').

\begin{theorem}[Conservation of Value in Pure Circulation under Skew-Symmetric Markups]
Let $\mathcal{A} = \{1, 2, \dots, N\}$ be a closed system of trading agents. Prior to trade, agent $\alpha \in \mathcal{A}$ holds commodity bundle $\mathbf{q}_\alpha \in \bigoplus_{i \in \mathcal{T}_0} \mathcal{U}_i$ and money stock $m_\alpha \mathbf{e}_m \in \mathcal{U}_m$, with initial total value:
\begin{equation}
    V_\alpha(0) = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{T}_0} \lambda_i q_{\alpha, i} + \lambda_m m_\alpha
\end{equation}
Suppose any pair of agents $(\alpha, \beta)$ executes bilateral trades of commodity $i$ in physical volume $X_{\alpha \beta}^{(i)} = -X_{\beta \alpha}^{(i)}$ ($X_{\alpha \beta}^{(i)} > 0$ denoting net sale from $\alpha$ to $\beta$) at an arbitrary negotiated market price $p_{\alpha \beta}^{(i)} = \frac{\lambda_i}{\lambda_m}(1 + \delta_{\alpha \beta}^{(i)})$, where $\delta_{\alpha \beta}^{(i)} \in (-1, \infty)$ is an arbitrary bilateral markup/markdown parameter. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
    \item \textbf{Individual Value Shift:} Agent $\alpha$'s post-exchange value changes by:
    \begin{equation}
        \Delta V_\alpha = V_\alpha(1) - V_\alpha(0) = \sum_{\beta \in \mathcal{A}} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{T}_0} \lambda_i \delta_{\alpha \beta}^{(i)} X_{\alpha \beta}^{(i)}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Global Conservation Invariance:} Across the entire economy (or any closed trading sub-coalition), aggregate value is strictly invariant:
    \begin{equation}
        \Delta V_{\text{total}} = \sum_{\alpha \in \mathcal{A}} \Delta V_\alpha \equiv 0
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Zero-Sum Uniform Surcharge:} If every seller imposes a uniform proportional markup $\delta_{\alpha \beta}^{(i)} = \delta > 0$ when selling and pays $\delta$ when buying with balanced trade ($\sum_{i, \beta} \lambda_i X_{\alpha \beta}^{(i)} = 0$), then $\Delta V_\alpha = 0$ for every individual agent $\alpha \in \mathcal{A}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
When $\alpha$ sells $X_{\alpha \beta}^{(i)}$ units of commodity $i$ to $\beta$ at price $p_{\alpha \beta}^{(i)}$, $\alpha$ surrenders commodity value $\lambda_i X_{\alpha \beta}^{(i)}$ and receives money value $\lambda_m p_{\alpha \beta}^{(i)} X_{\alpha \beta}^{(i)} = \lambda_i (1 + \delta_{\alpha \beta}^{(i)}) X_{\alpha \beta}^{(i)}$, yielding a net bilateral value transfer $\pi_{\alpha \beta}^{(i)} = \lambda_i \delta_{\alpha \beta}^{(i)} X_{\alpha \beta}^{(i)}$. For counterparty $\beta$, the net value change is $\pi_{\beta \alpha}^{(i)} = -\lambda_i \delta_{\alpha \beta}^{(i)} X_{\alpha \beta}^{(i)} = -\pi_{\alpha \beta}^{(i)}$. Thus, for each commodity $i$, the value-transfer matrix $\Pi^{(i)} = [\pi_{\alpha \beta}^{(i)}]_{\alpha, \beta \in \mathcal{A}}$ is strictly skew-symmetric: $(\Pi^{(i)})^\top = -\Pi^{(i)}$. Summing over all agents yields:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta V_{\text{total}} = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{T}_0} \mathbf{1}^\top \Pi^{(i)} \mathbf{1} = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{T}_0} \mathbf{1}^\top \left(\Pi^{(i)} + \left(\Pi^{(i)}\right)^\top\right) \mathbf{1} = 0
\end{equation}
proving that pure circulation is a strictly conservative (measure-preserving) operator on total value.
\end{proof}

\section{Labor-Power vs. Living Labor: Stock-Flow Operator Splitting (Chapter 6)}

To satisfy the boundary-value problem of Chapter 5---where surplus-value must be generated while obeying strict equivalence ($\delta_{\alpha \beta} \equiv 0$) at every market node---the capitalist must find on the market a commodity whose \textbf{Use-Value is itself the active source of Value}. Marx resolves this via a rigorous dimensional distinction between \textbf{Labor-Power} (\textit{Arbeitskraft}, a stock commodity) and \textbf{Living Labor} (\textit{Arbeit}, a continuous flow operator).

\begin{definition}[Labor-Power $lp \in \mathcal{T}_0$ and its Stock Reproduction Value $\lambda_{lp}$]
Let $lp \in \mathcal{T}_0$ denote the commodity \textbf{Labor-Power}, sold for a discrete contract period (1 working day) under the \textbf{Double Freedom Precondition} (the worker $\omega$ possesses legal self-ownership over $\mathcal{U}_{lp}$ but holds zero means of production, $\mathbf{q}_\omega^{\text{means}} = \mathbf{0}$). Let $\mathbf{b} = (b_1, b_2, \dots, b_n)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^n_{\ge 0}$ be the culturally and historically determined daily subsistence bundle required to reproduce the worker's capacity, family, and skill. The unit stock value of Labor-Power is the inner product:
\begin{equation}
    \lambda_{lp} = V(\mathbf{b}) = \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\top \mathbf{b} = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{T}_0} \lambda_i b_i \quad \text{[measured in hours/day]}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Living Labor as a Value-Generating Flow Operator $\hat{\mathcal{L}}_T$]
The consumption of the use-value of Labor-Power inside the production process $\mathcal{P}$ over a working day of duration $T \in (0, T_{\max}]$ is \textbf{Living Labor}---a continuous flow operator $\hat{\mathcal{L}}_T$ projecting concrete labor through the Real Abstraction Operator $\mathcal{A}$ at normalized socially average intensity $\iota(t) \equiv 1$:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta V(T) = \operatorname{Use-Value}(lp)\big|_{[0, T]} = \int_0^T \iota(t) \, dt = T \quad \text{[measured in hours/day]}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Resolution of the Conservation Contradiction via Stock-Flow Defect]
Under strict equivalence of exchange in all commodity and labor markets ($\delta_{\alpha \beta} \equiv 0$), the net surplus-value $s$ generated per worker per day is the \textbf{Stock-Flow Defect} between the flow action of $\hat{\mathcal{L}}_T$ and the stock reproduction value $\lambda_{lp}$:
\begin{equation}
    s(T, \mathbf{b}, \boldsymbol{\lambda}) = \Delta V(T) - \lambda_{lp} = \int_0^T \iota(t) \, dt - \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\top \mathbf{b} = T - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{T}_0} \lambda_i b_i
\end{equation}
Whenever social productivity satisfies $\boldsymbol{\lambda}^\top \mathbf{b} < T$, strictly positive surplus-value $s > 0$ is generated without violating equivalent exchange at any boundary of circulation.
\end{theorem}

\section{Bifurcation of the Production Process: Constant vs. Variable Capital (Chapters 7--9)}

Consider the production of $Q_j$ units of commodity $j$ over 1 working day using physical means of production $\mathbf{a}_j = (a_{1j}, \dots, a_{nj})^\top \in \mathbb{R}^n_{\ge 0}$ and $L_j > 0$ living workers each working $T$ hours/day.

\begin{proposition}[Dual Operator Action of Concrete vs. Abstract Labor in Production]
During the production interval $[0, T]$, living labor executes two simultaneous, orthogonal mathematical operations:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item \textbf{Value-Preservation Homomorphism (Constant Capital $c_j$):} Through its \textit{concrete useful} character, labor preserves and transfers the pre-existing value of the consumed means of production $\mathbf{a}_j$ into the output $Q_j \mathbf{e}_j$ as an exact scalar invariant:
    \begin{equation}
        c_j = V_{\text{transferred}}(\mathbf{a}_j) = \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\top \mathbf{a}_j = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{T}_0} \lambda_i a_{ij} \implies \Delta c_j \equiv 0
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Value-Dilation Operator (Variable Capital $v_j$):} Through its \textit{abstract social} character, living labor injects fresh value $L_j T$, replacing its advanced variable capital $v_j = L_j \lambda_{lp}$ and generating surplus-value $s_j$:
    \begin{equation}
        v_j \xrightarrow{\hat{\mathcal{L}}_T} v_j + s_j = L_j \lambda_{lp} + L_j \left(T - \lambda_{lp}\right) = L_j T \implies \Delta v_j = s_j = L_j \left(T - \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\top \mathbf{b}\right) > 0
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
The total value of output $C'_j = Q_j \mathbf{e}_j$ is the affine functional in $T$:
\begin{equation}
    W_j(T) = c_j + v_j + s_j(T) = \underbrace{\boldsymbol{\lambda}^\top \mathbf{a}_j}_{\text{Dead Labor } (c_j)} + \underbrace{L_j \left(\boldsymbol{\lambda}^\top \mathbf{b}\right)}_{\text{Necessary Labor } (v_j)} + \underbrace{L_j \left(T - \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\top \mathbf{b}\right)}_{\text{Surplus Labor } (s_j)}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{definition}[Rate of Surplus-Value / Rate of Exploitation $e$]
The \textbf{Rate of Surplus-Value} $e$ is the fractional dilation of variable capital alone:
\begin{equation}
    e \coloneqq \frac{\Delta v}{v} = \frac{s}{v} = \frac{T - \lambda_{lp}}{\lambda_{lp}} = \frac{T - \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\top \mathbf{b}}{\boldsymbol{\lambda}^\top \mathbf{b}} = \frac{t_s}{t_n}
\end{equation}
partitioning the working day $T = t_n + t_s$ into Necessary Labor Time $t_n = \frac{T}{1+e}$ and Surplus Labor Time $t_s = \frac{e}{1+e}T$.
\end{definition}

\section{Differential Geometry of Exploitation, the Working Day, and the Three Laws of Surplus-Value Mass (Chapters 10--11)}

\subsection{Total Differential Decomposition of Exploitation Strategies}

Taking the total differential of $e(T, \boldsymbol{\lambda}, \mathbf{b}) = \frac{T}{\boldsymbol{\lambda}^\top \mathbf{b}} - 1$ on the production manifold yields:
\begin{equation}
    de = \underbrace{\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{lp}}\right) dT}_{\substack{\text{Absolute Surplus-Value} \\ \text{(Ch. 10: Working Day Elongation)}}} + \underbrace{\left(-\frac{T}{\lambda_{lp}^2} \mathbf{b}^\top d\boldsymbol{\lambda}\right)}_{\substack{\text{Relative Surplus-Value} \\ \text{(Ch. 12: Productivity Revolution)}}} + \underbrace{\left(-\frac{T}{\lambda_{lp}^2} \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\top d\mathbf{b}\right)}_{\substack{\text{Real Wage Compression} \\ \text{(Depression below } \lambda_{lp}\text{)}}}
\end{equation}

\subsection{The Antinomy of the Working Day and Biological Spoliation (Chapter 10)}

In Chapter 10, Marx shows that when the working day $T$ exceeds a physiological regeneration threshold $T_{\text{phys}}$, overwork induces premature exhaustion and shortens the worker's productive lifespan. We formalize this via the \textbf{True Biological Reproduction Cost Functional}:
\begin{equation}
    \Lambda_{lp}^{\text{true}}(T) = \lambda_{lp} + \gamma \max\!\left(0, \, T - T_{\text{phys}}\right)^2, \quad \gamma > 0
\end{equation}
Whenever $T > T_{\text{phys}}$ while the daily wage remains fixed at $\lambda_{lp}$, capital covertly liquidates the worker's biological stock ($\Lambda_{lp}^{\text{true}}(T) - \lambda_{lp} > 0$), violating long-run equivalent exchange. Because ``between equal rights, force decides,'' the working day $T(t) \in (\lambda_{lp}, T_{\max})$ evolves via the \textbf{Class-Struggle Differential Game}:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{T} = \kappa_K \frac{\partial s}{\partial T} (T_{\max} - T) - \kappa_W \frac{\partial \Lambda_{lp}^{\text{true}}}{\partial T} (T - \lambda_{lp}) = \kappa_K (T_{\max} - T) - 2 \kappa_W \gamma \max\!\left(0, \, T - T_{\text{phys}}\right)(T - \lambda_{lp})
\end{equation}
where $\kappa_K > 0$ and $\kappa_W > 0$ denote the bargaining/coercive force parameters of Capital and Labor/State factory legislation, respectively.

\subsection{Marx's Three Laws of the Mass of Surplus-Value (Chapter 11)}

\begin{theorem}[The Three Laws of Chapter 11 and the Absolute Substitution Barrier]
Let an enterprise employ $L$ workers with variable capital per worker $\lambda_{lp}$ and working day $T \le T_{\max} < 24\text{ hrs}$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
    \item \textbf{First Law (Proportionality to Variable Capital \& Rate of Exploitation):} The aggregate Mass of Surplus-Value $S$ is given by:
    \begin{equation}
        S(L, T, \lambda_{lp}) = V_{\text{total}} \cdot e = L \cdot \lambda_{lp} \left(\frac{T - \lambda_{lp}}{\lambda_{lp}}\right) = L \left(T - \lambda_{lp}\right)
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Second Law (Iso-Surplus Compensation Differential):} Along an iso-surplus manifold $S(L, T) = S_0$, a reduction in the living workforce $dL < 0$ (via mechanization) requires a compensating relative increase in the working day (or rate of exploitation):
    \begin{equation}
        dS = (T - \lambda_{lp}) dL + L \, dT = 0 \implies \frac{dT}{T - \lambda_{lp}} = -\frac{dL}{L}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Third Law (Absolute Physical Barrier to Labor-Saving Substitution):} Because $T$ is bounded above by $T_{\max} < 24\text{ hrs}$, there exists a strictly positive critical workforce threshold:
    \begin{equation}
        L_{\text{crit}} \coloneqq \frac{S_0}{T_{\max} - \lambda_{lp}} > 0
    \end{equation}
    such that for any $L < L_{\text{crit}}$, $S(L, T) < S_0$ for all admissible $T \in (\lambda_{lp}, T_{\max}]$. That is, beyond $L_{\text{crit}}$, no extension of the working day or intensification of exploitation can compensate for the expulsion of living labor from production.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Parts (1) and (2) follow immediately from definition and total differentiation of $S(L, T) = L(T - \lambda_{lp})$. For Part (3), since $T \le T_{\max}$, we have $S(L, T) = L(T - \lambda_{lp}) \le L(T_{\max} - \lambda_{lp})$. If $L < L_{\text{crit}} = \frac{S_0}{T_{\max} - \lambda_{lp}}$, then $S(L, T) \le L(T_{\max} - \lambda_{lp}) < S_0$, establishing the absolute upper bound that underpins the Tendency of the Rate of Profit to Fall in Volume III.
\end{proof}
